Module 1: The Endocrine System
Topic: System Overview & Gland Function

Learn Something:

Communication Method: The endocrine system uses chemical messengers called hormones released into the bloodstream to communicate over long distances.

Speed vs. Persistence: Unlike the nervous system, which reacts in milliseconds, the endocrine system reacts slowly (seconds to days) but its effects can last for weeks.

The Master Regulators: The Hypothalamus links the nervous and endocrine systems and drives the system. The Pituitary Gland (pea-sized at the base of the brain) receives signals from the hypothalamus and releases eight major hormones that often trigger other glands.

Major Glands: * Thyroid: Controls metabolism.

Adrenal Glands: Located on top of kidneys; manage stress response and blood pressure via hormones like epinephrine and cortisol.

Pancreas: Regulates blood sugar using insulin (lowers glucose) and glucagon (raises glucose).

Module 2: The Heart
Topic: Anatomy & The Cardiac Cycle

Learn Something:

Heart Wall Layers: The heart consists of three layers: the epicardium (outer), myocardium (middle muscular layer that powers pumping), and endocardium (inner lining).

Chambers: The heart has four chambers. The atria (upper) receive blood, while the ventricles (lower) pump it out.

Valves: Four valves ensure one-way blood flow. Atrioventricular (AV) valves (tricuspid and mitral) are between atria and ventricles; Semilunar valves (pulmonary and aortic) manage flow out of the heart.

The Cardiac Cycle: * Systole: The period of contraction where the heart pumps blood.

Diastole: The period of relaxation where chambers fill with blood.

Cardiac Output (CO): Calculated as Stroke Volume (SV) x Heart Rate (HR).

Module 3: Blood Vessels
Topic: Structure & Hemodynamics

Learn Something:

Vessel Types: Arteries carry blood away from the heart, Veins return blood to the heart, and Capillaries are the microscopic sites of gas and nutrient exchange.

Tunics (Layers): Vessel walls have three layers: tunica externa (outer), tunica media (middle smooth muscle for vasodilation/vasoconstriction), and tunica intima (smooth inner layer).

Arteries vs. Veins: Arteries have thicker, more muscular walls to handle high pressure. Veins have thinner walls and contain one-way valves to prevent blood from flowing backward under low pressure.

Capillaries: Their walls are only one cell thick, allowing oxygen and nutrients to diffuse into tissues.

Module 4: Lymphatic & Immune Systems
Topic: Fluid Balance & Defense

Learn Something:

Fluid Recovery: The lymphatic system reabsorbs excess interstitial fluid (about 3 liters per day) and returns it to the bloodstream to maintain fluid balance.

Lymph Nodes: These bean-shaped organs act as filters that cleanse lymph of pathogens and store immune cells (lymphocytes).

Major Organs:

Spleen: The largest lymphatic organ; it filters blood and removes old red blood cells.

Thymus: Where T cells (immune cells) mature.

Bone Marrow: Produces all white blood cells.

Movement: Unlike the heart, lymph is not actively pumped; it is moved by skeletal muscle contraction and breathing.
