In the common pathway of coagulation:
   1. Prothrombinase converts prothrombin → thrombin.

   2. Thrombin then converts fibrinogen into fibrin.

   3. Fibrin forms the mesh that stabilizes the blood clot.


Hemostasis occurs in three main steps:
      1. Vascular spasm – the injured vessel constricts to reduce blood loss.

      2. Platelet plug formation – platelets adhere and form a temporary plug.

      3. Coagulation – a stable clot forms through the clotting cascade.

Immunity


Type I hypersensitivity – IgE-mediated allergies (e.g., anaphylaxis, hay fever).


Type II hypersensitivity – antibodies attack cells directly (e.g., hemolytic anemia).


Type III hypersensitivity – immune complex deposition (e.g., lupus).


Type IV hypersensitivity – T-cell mediated (delayed) hypersensitivity

Steps of immune cell movement to infection


1️⃣ Chemotaxis
Cells move toward chemical signals.


2️⃣ Adhesion (CAM binding)
Leukocytes stick to blood vessel walls using CAMs.


3️⃣ Diapedesis
Cells squeeze between endothelial cells to leave the bloodstream.


4️⃣ Phagocytosis
They attack pathogens.


complement system
1️⃣ A pathogen enters the body.
2️⃣ Antibodies (usually IgG or IgM) bind to the pathogen's Antigen.
3️⃣ The complement protein Complement protein C1 binds to the antibody–antigen complex.
4️⃣ This triggers a cascade reaction activating other complement proteins.
